1. NFL Wide Receivers vs. Tight Ends: Statistical Analysis
Project Overview:
This group project focused on analyzing NFL wide receivers (WRs) and tight ends (TEs) to determine which statistical factors contribute most to receiving success. Using statistical analysis, we examined relationships between receiving yards, targets, receptions, touchdowns, and yards per reception. Our goal was to identify whether player performance was primarily influenced by position, efficiency, or overall involvement in the offense.

Key Statistical Findings:
Correlation Analysis: Receiving yards had strong positive relationships with first downs (r = 0.9621), targets (r = 0.9314), and receptions (r = 0.9161).
Logistic Regression: Identified a statistically significant relationship between yards per reception and player position (p < 0.0001).
Multiple Linear Regression: Developed a model explaining approximately 98.3% of the variation in receiving yards (R² = 0.9834).
Key Performance Drivers: Targets, yards per target, and touchdowns were significant predictors of total receiving yards.

What I Learned:
This project strengthened my understanding of correlation analysis, hypothesis testing, logistic regression, and multiple linear regression. I learned how to interpret statistical results, evaluate relationships between variables, and use data to support conclusions. Most importantly, our findings suggested that receiving success is influenced more by a player's involvement in the offense than by their position alone.
Tools Used: JMP (Statistical Analysis Software) and Microsoft PowerPoint.

2. R Programming: Web Scraping with the rvest Package
Project Overview:
This group project focused on exploring the rvest package in R, which is used for web scraping and extracting information from websites. Our goal was to understand how R can collect, organize, and analyze data directly from online sources. We demonstrated the package's capabilities by extracting information about Star Wars movies, including titles, release dates, directors, and movie descriptions, and organizing the results into a structured dataset.

Key Skills and Findings:
Web Scraping: Used the rvest package to extract HTML content and convert website information into structured data.
R Programming: Explored functions such as read_html(), html_elements(), html_text2(), and html_table() to collect and organize data.
Data Analysis: Organized information from seven Star Wars films and identified that George Lucas directed four of the seven movies in the dataset.
Data Collection Limitations: Learned how website layout changes, JavaScript content, and site restrictions can affect web scraping.

What I Learned:
This project strengthened my understanding of R programming, web scraping, and data collection techniques. I learned how to extract unstructured website information and transform it into organized datasets for analysis. Additionally, I gained experience working with R packages, interpreting extracted data, and understanding the importance of accurate and responsible data collection.
Tools Used: R, RStudio, and Microsoft PowerPoint.

4. Marketing Analytics: Social Media & Advertising Performance
Project Overview:
This group project focused on analyzing the effectiveness of different advertising platforms on Chipotle's sales performance. Using marketing mix modeling and regression analysis, we examined how advertising spending across social media, television, banner ads, and podcasts influenced sales. Our primary goal was to identify which advertising channels contributed most to sales and determine how Chipotle could strategically allocate an additional $10,000 in advertising spending to maximize predicted sales.

Key Statistical Findings:
Regression Analysis: Developed a multiple linear regression model to evaluate the relationship between advertising spending and sales performance.
Model Performance: Achieved an adjusted R² of 0.655, indicating that the model explained approximately 65.5% of the variation in sales.
Advertising Effectiveness: Identified banner ads, Facebook, Instagram, and podcasts as significant predictors of sales in the final model.
Budget Optimization: Allocated an additional $10,000 across four advertising channels, producing a projected total sales figure of approximately $650,741.

What I Learned:
This project strengthened my understanding of marketing analytics, regression modeling, hypothesis testing, and data-driven budget allocation. I learned how to interpret statistical results, identify effective advertising channels, and use quantitative analysis to support marketing decisions. Most importantly, I gained experience applying analytical techniques to a realistic business scenario involving advertising strategy and sales performance.
Tools Used: Microsoft Excel (Regression Analysis and Budget Allocation).


5. Hurricane Sandy: Media Bias Analysis
Project Overview:
This group presentation examined the devastating impact of Hurricane Sandy in 2012, which caused approximately $70 billion in damages, particularly affecting New York and New Jersey. Our goal was to analyze how news articles, government reports, and third-party sources presented information about the storm and how different cognitive biases influenced public perception, preparedness, and recovery efforts.

Key Biases Identified:
Confirmation Bias: Reinforcing existing beliefs about climate change and infrastructure weaknesses.
Framing Bias: Presenting information in ways that influence public opinion.
Anchoring Bias: Using terms like "Superstorm Sandy" to shape initial perceptions.
Planning Fallacy: Underestimating the time required for recovery and rebuilding.
Bounded Awareness: Focusing on immediate destruction while overlooking long-term consequences.

What I Learned:
This project strengthened my ability to identify biases, critically evaluate information from multiple sources, and understand how media coverage can influence public decision-making. I also gained a better understanding of Hurricane Sandy's economic and social consequences.
Tools Used: Microsoft Excel, PowerPoint, and secondary research.
